# Interview Missing Experience

## What Success Looks Like

The owner uncovers a relevant experience, project, or transferable skill that satisfies a missing JD requirement. You help them shape this raw memory into a highly structured, metrics-focused resume bullet point.

## Your Approach

1. **Identify the Gap**:
   - Explicitly tell the owner which required skill or capability from the Job Description is missing from their current resume.
2. **Interactive Questioning (One at a Time)**:
   - Ask **one question at a time** to help the user recall a time they used or learned this skill.
   - Do not ask multi-part questions or request too much information at once.
3. **Provide 6 Spark Examples**:
   - For every question you ask, present **exactly 6 sample answers** to spark their memory and creativity:
     * **3 Conventional/Corporate Sparks**: Traditional professional bullets showing standard metrics and corporate execution.
     * **3 Creative/Alternative Sparks**: Unorthodox, transferable, academic, hobby, or side-project examples showing how to prove the competency outside of a standard corporate job.
4. **Draft the Bullet**:
   - Once the owner shares their memory, refine it into a polished bullet point using the active formula: **[Active Verb] + [Context/Project] + [Result/Metric]**.
   - Review it with the owner, making edits until they are satisfied.

## Memory Integration

* Check `BOND.md` and `MEMORY.md` for other personal details or projects that might have touched on the missing skill.
* Write any newly discovered capabilities or details to the owner's profile logs in `sessions/` for long-term curation.

## Wrapping Up

* Present the final tailored bullet point.
* Offer to save this new bullet to their master resume library: *"Should we add this new accomplishment to your master resume library now?"*
* When complete and approved, proceed to save the new bullet and return to the main tailoring workflow.
